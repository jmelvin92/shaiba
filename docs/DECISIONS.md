# Decisions Log — Shaiba

Append-only log of decisions with reasoning, so future sessions don't re-litigate or accidentally reverse them. Newest at the bottom. Format: date, decision, why, alternatives rejected.

---

**2026-08-13 — Angled top-down perspective camera, not true orthographic isometric.**
A perspective camera pitched ~50–55° with a narrow FOV (~35°) gives the "almost isometric" diorama feel Joshua asked for while keeping depth cues (parallax, dune scale) that pure orthographic loses in an open desert. Rejected: orthographic (flattens dunes), free third-person camera (wrong genre feel).

**2026-08-13 — Chunked streamed terrain from the first terrain implementation (Phase 4).**
The game is open-world on a large map; retrofitting chunking onto a monolithic terrain later would mean rebuilding terrain, collision, prop scattering, and the sand-deformation integration twice. Cost of chunking early is modest; cost of retrofitting is a rewrite.

**2026-08-13 — Sand footprints via world-space deformation texture + vertex-displacement shader, not geometry edits or decals.**
Editing chunk meshes per footstep fights the streaming system and is expensive; plain decals can't depress the surface so prints look painted-on at an angled camera. A SubViewport-accumulated deformation texture is the standard, cheap, fade-able approach. To be validated with a prototype at the start of Phase 5.

**2026-08-13 — Typed GDScript only; no C# unless profiling forces it.**
One language keeps every session's code uniform and godot-mcp-friendly; typed GDScript catches most novice mistakes at parse time. If a hot path (likely chunk generation) ever profiles too slow, revisit here.

**2026-08-13 — Repo root = Godot project root; .blend sources committed alongside .glb exports.**
godot-mcp and version control cover everything with zero path indirection; committed .blend files keep every asset re-editable in later sessions. Git LFS deferred until repo size demands it (~1 GB threshold).

**2026-08-13 — One autoload (`Game`) to start; new autoloads require a logged justification.**
Global singletons are the main spaghetti vector in small Godot projects. Signals + composition first; the log entry requirement forces the discussion.

**2026-08-13 — GitHub repo `jmelvin92/shaiba`, private; `main` (stable) + `development` (integration) + per-phase feature branches, `--no-ff` merges.**
Joshua asked for main to stay protected until testing passes; --no-ff keeps each phase's work legible as one unit in history. Private by default — flip to public anytime.

## Phase 2 — camera & movement

**2026-08-13 — Movement tuning, first pass: max_speed 4.6 m/s, acceleration 26 m/s², friction 32 m/s², turn_speed 11.**
Measured on the graybox: full speed in ~0.18 s, stop in 0.15 s. 4.6 m/s is a brisk walk for a 1.75 m character, leaving headroom for a run speed in Phase 3. Superseded below after Joshua played it.

**2026-08-13 — Movement tuning, after playtest: acceleration 16, friction 24, turn_speed 7, and a new `turn_drag` of 0.55. Speed unchanged at 4.6 m/s.**
Joshua's note was "more weight, especially during sudden turns or launches." Lowering acceleration covers launches, but lowering `turn_speed` alone would only have slowed the *visible* rotation — velocity still changed direction the instant you pressed a key, so a reversal would look heavier without feeling it. `turn_drag` fixes that by scaling available acceleration with how well the body already faces where you asked to go: aligned gives full thrust, a full about-face gives `1 - turn_drag`, recovering as the body comes round. Measured after: launch to 90% of top speed 0.27 s (was 0.16), stop 0.20 s, a 180° reversal takes 0.40 s before you move back at all, and a right-angle turn dips to 71% of top speed so corners cost something. Slope, curb and staircase behaviour re-verified unchanged.

**2026-08-13 — `floor_max_angle` 32°, so slopes up to ~30° are walkable and steeper ones are not.**
PLAN's gate asks for ~30° walkable / steeper blocked. Setting the threshold slightly above 30 means the 30° test ramp is comfortably walkable rather than marginal, while the 40° and 50° ramps are firmly refused. Verified on all five graybox ramps.

**2026-08-13 — Step climbing by lifting the body *before* `move_and_slide`, not teleporting it onto the ledge after.**
The usual up→forward→down probe has to move the body forward by roughly the capsule radius to clear the obstacle's face, which pops it visibly. Instead, when a wall-like surface is within `step_probe_distance` (0.5 m) ahead and there is headroom, the body is raised by `max_step_height` and `move_and_slide`'s floor snap puts it back down in the same tick — onto the ledge once the body has cleared its face, otherwise straight back where it was. Nothing is visible until the step is actually taken. Consequence: `floor_snap_length` must exceed `max_step_height`, so `_ready` clamps it. Verified: 0.20 m and 0.35 m curbs climb, 0.50 m is refused, a 0.25 m staircase walks up cleanly. Walkable slopes are excluded from the probe so they keep their natural along-the-slope speed.

**2026-08-13 — Run is a held `sprint` action (shift) that raises the target speed, not a separate movement state. walk_speed 4.6, run_speed 7.4.**
Joshua wants walk and run as the two gaits, run on held shift. Implementing it as a target-speed swap means the existing acceleration, friction and turn_drag all apply unchanged — winding up to a run takes 0.18 s and dropping back the same, so the gait change has the same weight as everything else, and there is no state machine to keep in sync with the animation one. Phase 3's AnimationTree therefore blends idle↔walk↔run off the resulting planar speed rather than off an input flag, which also makes the blend correct while accelerating, on slopes, and when turn_drag is holding speed down. Re-verified at run speed: 15/30° ramps still walkable, 40/50° still refused, 0.20/0.35 m curbs still climb, 0.50 m still blocked — a faster capsule does not punch over anything. `max_speed` renamed to `walk_speed` while the meaning was still local to this file.

**2026-08-13 — Jump on space (1.1 m), with coyote time, a jump buffer, variable height and reduced air control.**
`jump_height` is expressed in metres and converted to a launch velocity against whatever gravity is in force, so retuning `gravity_scale` doesn't silently change the hop. The three forgivenesses are all standard and all cheap: 0.12 s of coyote time after leaving a ledge, a 0.12 s buffered press so hitting space just before landing still fires, and cutting the rise to 45% if you release early — measured, a held jump clears 1.15 m in 0.85 s and a tap 0.58 m in 0.55 s. `air_control` 0.35 applies to both acceleration and friction mid-air, so a jump largely commits you to the arc you launched with; without it you could hover-steer, which would undo the weight the ground movement just gained. Jumping while crouched is refused rather than auto-standing — one input, one meaning.

**2026-08-13 — Crouch on ctrl or C, held rather than toggled, and you cannot stand up under something.**
Held matches sprint, so both modifiers behave the same way. Bound to two keys because ctrl is awkward on a Mac trackpad. The capsule shrinks 1.75 → 1.15 m over ~0.05 s rather than snapping, with the collision shape and mesh kept sitting on the character's feet, and the shape and mesh resources are marked `resource_local_to_scene` so resizing one player could never resize another. Standing up is gated on sweeping the crouched capsule up through the space the standing one would occupy — exact, and it reuses `test_move` rather than hand-rolling a query. Verified under a 1.4 m ceiling: crouch holds after the key is released and only springs back once clear.

**2026-08-13 — `graybox.tscn` gained fixtures for the new abilities.**
A test level that can't exercise an ability isn't testing it. Added a 1.3 m-clearance crouch tunnel, a 0.9 m ledge (jumpable) beside a 1.6 m one (not), and a 4.0 m gap between two platforms that a walk cannot clear but a sprint can. Each was verified by script to behave as intended, which is also a check on the tuning: if a later change breaks the jump, the gap stops being crossable.

**2026-08-13 — Default camera distance 23 m (was 18), zoom range 9–34 m.**
Joshua wanted the camera further out by default. 23 m puts the character at roughly 12% of screen height — small enough to read the terrain around them, which suits an open desert, and the clamps were widened so there is still real travel in both directions from the new default.

**2026-08-13 — Camera obstruction, first attempt: duck the camera in along its arm (own `ShapeCast3D`, not `SpringArm3D`).**
Kept the player visible and the camera out of geometry, but at a 52° pitch there is no distance that both frames the scene nicely and sees a player standing 2 m from a 6 m wall, so the view went cramped. **Superseded below** — Joshua played it and asked for the camera to stop moving.

**2026-08-13 — The camera never moves to avoid geometry. Obstructing objects fade instead (`scenes/camera/occluder_fader.gd`).**
This is what the previous entry said the real answer was, brought forward because the ducking was as intrusive in practice as predicted. The camera now holds its zoom distance unconditionally; an `OccluderFader` child of the rig traces sight-lines from the camera to three heights on the character, walking each line hit-by-hit rather than stopping at the first, and fades everything it finds to 22% opacity. Fading uses `GeometryInstance3D.transparency`, a per-instance multiplier — no duplicated materials, no changes to the palette `.tres` files, and it needs Forward+, which we are on. Ducking is now impossible to trigger: with the camera 18 m above the character, only something taller than that could reach it. Cost is ~18 raycasts a tick, which is nothing.

**2026-08-13 — Fading is opt-in via collision layer 3, so terrain can never dissolve.**
The fader masks layer 3 only. Ground and (from Phase 4) terrain chunks sit on layer 1 alone and are therefore never candidates — important, because at this camera angle a tall dune between the camera and the player would otherwise fade the world out from under them. Props and buildings opt in by sitting on layers 1 and 3 (`collision_layer = 5`). The fader also resolves a hit body to its meshes by checking the body itself, then its descendants, then its parent's — so it works both for CSG shapes, which are their own geometry, and for imported props where the collider and the mesh are siblings.

**2026-08-13 — `physics/common/physics_interpolation = true`; player and camera both update in `_physics_process`.**
Both move on the same 60 Hz tick, so there is no relative jitter between them, and interpolation smooths the pair together to the render rate. Measured while walking at 120 fps: the player's screen position holds to within 0.03 px of a straight line over 180 frames.

**2026-08-13 — `graybox.tscn` is built from `CSGBox3D` nodes with `use_collision`.**
One node per block instead of a StaticBody3D + MeshInstance3D + CollisionShape3D trio, which keeps a 30-block test level readable and editable. CSG is documented as a prototyping tool and a graybox is exactly that; if it ever costs frames it becomes a baked mesh.

**2026-08-13 — Collision layers: 1 = world/terrain, 2 = player.**
The camera's obstruction cast masks layer 1 only, so it can never be blocked by the player it is framing.

**2026-08-13 — `level_root.gd` is shared by `world.tscn` and `graybox.tscn`, and the lighting lives in `desert_environment.tscn`.**
A deliberate exception to "one script per scene": both level scenes need the identical six lines of wiring (tell the rig what to follow, tell the player which way the camera faces), which is the second real user the conventions ask for before extracting. Same reasoning for the environment: one place to do Phase 7's lighting pass instead of two copies to keep in sync.

**2026-08-13 — Camera pitch lowered 52° → 45° for a more cinematic angle; Joshua's request in a follow-up pass.**
At 52° the graybox read almost like a flat map — block tops and shadows, no vertical faces. At 45° walls and ledges show their faces and the scene gains real depth, while the framing still reads as the angled top-down diorama (screenshot-compared 52/45/40 from the same spawn; 40° was noticeably more dramatic and is one export-var away if wanted). The occluder fading was re-verified at 45°: player spawned 2 m behind the 6 m TallWall, wall ghosted correctly with the player fully visible. The original 50–55° range in PLAN's fixed decisions is superseded by this entry. Note for screenshot automation: check `pgrep -fl Godot` for stale game processes before trusting a window capture — a leftover debug window from an earlier session produced identical-looking "before/after" screenshots and cost a debugging round.

**2026-08-13 — The protagonist is AI-generated (Meshy.ai), not hand-modelled, and the whole conditioning pass is a committed script.**
Joshua generated a rigged, animated desert traveler in Meshy and downloaded one glb holding the mesh, a 24-bone rig and seven motion clips. That skips the modelling this phase was scoped for, but an as-downloaded asset is not a game asset: it faced the wrong way, its jump carried 3.65 m of forward travel, its material ignored light entirely, and it had no fall, land or crouch idle. All of that is fixed in `tools/build_player.py`, run headless against `assets/blender/player_source.glb` (committed) to produce `player.blend` and `player.glb`. Scripting it rather than clicking through Blender is what makes the quality gate's "re-export reproduces the glb" claim true, and it doubles as the documentation of what was done to the asset.

**2026-08-13 — The character keeps its baked texture. A deliberate exception to the palette-only rule.**
ART_DIRECTION says palette materials and no textures. Joshua chose to keep Meshy's baked texture rather than have it rebuilt with flat palette materials, on the grounds that the model already reads as a desert traveler in sand and cream tones. The exception is the *colour source* only — the material settings were still corrected (below), and the rule stands for every asset built from scratch. Worth revisiting if the character ends up looking foreign next to Phase 6's hand-made house and camel; a screenshot at the gameplay angle beside those props is the test.

**2026-08-13 — The material is made opaque, lit and matte even though the texture stays.**
As exported, Meshy's material was `alphaMode: BLEND`, double-sided, fully metallic, roughness 0.41, and — the real problem — `emissiveFactor [1,1,1]` with a full emissive map, which makes the character self-lit. It would have rendered as a flat cut-out that ignored the warm sun, cast no believable shadow, sorted badly against terrain and fought the occluder fader's own transparency. The build script zeroes emission and metallic, sets roughness 1.0 to match the palette `.tres` look, makes it opaque and single-sided, and deletes the emissive map. Verified in-game: the character now takes the directional light and casts a proper shadow. Dropping the emissive map and halving the base colour to 1024² also took the glb from 11 MB to 2.2 MB, which is why the repo is nowhere near the LFS threshold.

**2026-08-13 — The character keeps 9,300 tris against a 2,500-tri budget.**
Joshua chose not to decimate. One character at 9.3k costs nothing on this machine, and decimating a skinned mesh risks pulling the weights around. The budget still governs anything we model ourselves; this is a note that the number in ART_DIRECTION is a target for hand-made assets, not a ceiling enforced on imports. Revisit if crowds of NPCs ever appear.

**2026-08-13 — Movement speeds are retuned to the animations' own strides. Supersedes the Phase 2 tuning.**
The clips are paced for a real human: measured against the imported rig, the walk covers 1.40 m/s and the run 4.90 m/s. Phase 2's 4.6 / 7.4 were roughly three times faster, which would have meant playing the walk at 3.1× — frantic and obviously wrong. Offered the choice between stretching the clips and slowing the character, Joshua chose to slow the character, so `walk_speed` 4.6 → 1.4, `run_speed` 7.4 → 4.9, `crouch_speed` 2.0 → 1.0. `acceleration` 16 → 8 and `friction` 24 → 12 are rescaled to keep roughly Phase 2's *feel* (about 0.18 s to reach walking pace, a 0.6 s wind-up into a run) rather than its numbers. This is a real change to how the game plays and is the main thing to judge at playtest; every value is an `@export`, so reversing it is a number change. Knock-on: the graybox run-only gap was 4.0 m and a running jump now spans about 3.9 m, so the far platform moved to make the gap 2.8 m — still uncrossable at a walk (1.1 m). The 1.3 m crouch tunnel needed no change: the crouched traveler stands 1.19 m.

**2026-08-13 — The locomotion blend space is anchored at each clip's measured stride speed, which removes the need for any playback-rate correction.**
`tools/measure_gaits.gd` plays each clip against the real rig and measures how fast the planted foot travels backwards relative to the hips — that is the speed the clip "wants" to move at. Those numbers (0, 1.4, 4.9) are the blend-space anchor positions, and the blend position is simply the player's planar speed. Because idle is anchored at 0 m/s, blending it toward walk shortens the stride in proportion, so the blended pose's own ground speed always equals the blend position across the whole range. The usual `TimeScale` node correcting playback rate against speed is therefore unnecessary, and the tree is two blend spaces and three clips rather than a pair of nested blend trees. Measured in the running game by `tools/verify_player.gd`: the planted foot slips 14% of body speed at both walk and run. The two gaits agreeing to the same figure is the evidence that what remains is the foot rolling through its stance, not a stride mismatch.

**2026-08-13 — The animator tracks the state it asked for, rather than reading back the current one.**
`AnimationNodeStateMachinePlayback.travel()` only takes effect when the tree next processes, so `get_current_node()` still reports the previous state within the same tick. Driving off it meant the jump was cancelled the instant it was requested: `play_jump()` travelled to `jump`, then the same tick's `set_locomotion()` saw "still in locomotion, and airborne" and travelled to `fall`. The animator now remembers its own request, and a one-shot (jump or landing) holds the machine for that clip's length before locomotion may speak again. Caught by `tools/verify_player.gd`, which asserts the expected state after each input — not something a screenshot would ever have shown.

**2026-08-13 — Crouched movement holds a pose. A known gap, not an oversight.**
Meshy's `Cautious_Crouch_Walk_Right` is a lateral strafe: its planted foot travels 1.01 m/s sideways and 0.00 m/s forward. No rotation fixes that, because rotating the clip turns the facing and the travel together. The source set has no forward crouch walk, so `crouch_walk` is a held crouch pose and the feet slide while crouch-moving. Crouch is deliberately slow (1.0 m/s) to keep that quiet, and the Phase 3 gate asks for no foot-sliding at walk and run specifically. The crouch clips come from `CrouchLookAroundBow` instead, whose 1.10–1.19 m head height happens to match the 1.15 m crouch capsule almost exactly — which is why no collider or tunnel retuning was needed. The fix, when it matters, is a handful of extra Meshy clips (forward crouch walk, a real fall, a real landing).

**2026-08-13 — `assets/blender/` carries a `.gdignore`.**
Godot 4 imports `.blend` files natively and tried to, failing on a missing Blender path in headless mode, and would otherwise have imported the raw Meshy source as a second game asset. The `.gdignore` keeps the whole sources folder outside the import pipeline: `assets/blender/` is for editing, `assets/models/` is for the game.

**2026-08-13 — Stair climbing rebuilt: lift by the step's real height, smooth the mesh, and don't call a step a fall.**
Joshua reported stairs stopped feeling smooth once the character replaced the capsule. Tracing the body tick by tick showed why: `_try_step_up` always lifted by the full `max_step_height` (0.35 m) regardless of the actual tread, so the body ended each tick above the step with nothing under it, counted as airborne, and *fell* for about eight ticks. The character was hopping up every stair and — invisible on a capsule, obvious on a person — playing the fall animation on each one. Three changes. The probe now reaches only as far as this tick actually travels (looking half a metre ahead lifted the body while it was still clear of the step, which reads as floating), then measures the tread by raising a test transform, reaching forward past the capsule's own radius and dropping onto whatever is there, and lifts by exactly that plus 2 cm of clearance. A short grace after a step keeps the character counting as footed while it is at tread height but not yet over the tread, which is what stops the fall animation firing. And the mesh is held back and slid up over about 0.2 s, so the one-tick move the physics has to make is not one the eye has to: measured over the graybox staircase, the collider still moves 0.270 m in a tick but the rendered character never moves more than 0.063 m, roughly two pixels at the gameplay camera. The smoothing keys off the height the body *actually* gained, not the lift requested — a step is often attempted a tick or two before it takes, and absorbing the requested lift made the character visibly sink first.

**2026-08-13 — `walk_speed` raised 1.4 → 1.8 m/s at Joshua's request.**
Free to do: the blend anchors sit at each clip's measured stride speed, so any speed between 0 and the run's 4.9 m/s stays stride-matched automatically — 1.8 simply leans the blend a little toward the run and the gait reads as a brisk walk. Re-measured after the change: the planted foot still slips only 15% of body speed, unchanged from 1.4.

**2026-08-13 — Movement lightened: acceleration 8 → 12, friction 12 → 14, turn_drag 0.55 → 0.45.**
Joshua said the movement felt too heavy. Timing it (`tools/verify_player.gd --feel`) showed where: starting and stopping were already quicker than the Phase 2 figures he approved, but the **180° reversal had regressed to 0.53 s against the 0.40 s he signed off**, and the wind-up into a run had grown to 0.57 s. Both are side effects of the speed retune — acceleration was rescaled to preserve the time to reach *walking* pace, which left the much larger gap up to running pace taking far longer, and `turn_drag` costs a fixed fraction of thrust that bites harder when there is less thrust to spare. The new values restore the approved feel rather than inventing a new one: reversal 0.38 s, run wind-up 0.38 s, walk launch 0.15 s, stop 0.13 s. `turn_speed` is untouched at 7.0 — the complaint was about responding, not about how fast the body swings round.

## Phase 4 — terrain & chunk streaming

**2026-08-13 — The sand is a layer with depth, not just a heightfield: surface = base ground + sand depth.**
Joshua's expanded vision at the Phase 4 planning session: sand deep in some places and shallow in others, pushed down by feet, piled up by wind, remembering the trail you walked. All of that needs "how much sand is here" to be a first-class quantity, so the terrain is modelled as a hard substrate (`get_base_height`) under a sand layer of varying thickness (`get_sand_depth`), and the walkable surface is their sum. The dunes *are* the sand — dune bodies are metres deep, the flats between them a 0.15 m dusting over hard ground, which happens to be how real deserts work. Footprints (Phase 5) will cap their depth by the local sand depth, and the chunk mesh already carries normalised depth in vertex `COLOR.a` so the deformation shader gets that cap per-fragment for free.

**2026-08-13 — Terrain queries are analytic and live on `TerrainSettings` (resources/terrain/desert.tres).**
`get_surface_height` / `get_base_height` / `get_sand_depth` evaluate the noise fields directly instead of reading chunk data, so they work for any world position — loaded or not — are thread-safe, and can never disagree with the mesh builder, which samples the same functions. The documented caveat: between mesh vertices the collision surface is a straight edge while the analytic field curves, so they differ by up to the field's curvature over one metre (measured: ≤ 5 cm); anything needing exact collision height raycasts instead. A `get_deformed_height` is reserved by name for Phase 5 but probably never needs to exist, since deformation is visual-only.

**2026-08-13 — Chunk collision is HeightMapShape3D, and the visual mesh copies its cell diagonal.**
The purpose-built heightfield collider: map data is cheap to build off-thread (a trimesh BVH build is the classic main-thread hitch), an order of magnitude smaller in memory, and — verified by `verify_terrain --collision` raining 2,000 rays — it matches the rendered mesh to 0.0000 m because both triangulate each cell along the same (x+1,z)→(x,z+1) diagonal. Keeping analytic slopes under 31° (audited empirically; currently 26.4° worst) guarantees no collision triangle can present a wall-like face to the player's `_try_step_up`, which fires only above the 32° floor_max_angle — the Phase 3 watch-item, closed by construction. Deliberate steeper-than-walkable slip faces are off the table until something guards that probe.

**2026-08-13 — Flat shading comes from screen-space-derivative normals in the fragment shader, not split vertices.**
`sand_terrain.gdshader` computes each pixel's normal from position derivatives, which is constant per triangle — true facets — while the mesh keeps shared vertices (6× fewer). The decisive reason is Phase 5: displaced vertices get correct faceted lighting automatically, where baked normals would go stale the moment a footprint deforms the surface. Verified on this Mac's Metal driver with no precision artifacts. The same shader is where Phase 5 adds its deformation uniforms — a diff, not a material swap.

**2026-08-13 — Chunk edges are seamless by construction: vertices derive from global integer grid indices.**
A shared edge vertex is computed from the same integers by both neighbours, so their floats are bit-identical (asserted by `verify_terrain`'s seam check) and no skirts or welding exist anywhere. The rule the builder must never break: world coordinates come from `float(global_index) * cell_size`, never from accumulating offsets from the chunk origin.

**2026-08-13 — No far-field ring, no horizon skirt. The camera cannot see the streaming edge.**
At maximum zoom (34 m, 45° pitch, 35° FOV) every frustum ray that can hit ground does so within ~120 m of the player; everything further up-frame is sky. The 5×5 loaded grid keeps the nearest possible edge ≥ 160 m away, so the edge is unconditionally off-screen — verified by screenshot at max zoom. The far-field deliverable in PLAN.md is therefore satisfied by *nothing*, which bought the session time for the sand-depth work. Revisit only if camera pitch, zoom_max or FOV ever change.

**2026-08-13 — Streaming: WorkerThreadPool builds, mutex-guarded handoff, budgeted main-thread installs.**
One pool task per chunk runs the pure `TerrainChunk.build_data`; results queue behind a mutex and `_process` installs them under a 2 ms budget (at least one per frame), so a burst of finished chunks cannot own a frame. Tasks can't be cancelled mid-run, so a chunk that leaves range while building is dropped at install time instead. Requests go out nearest-first; load radius 2 (5×5), unload radius 3. One real bug from the first threaded run: creating the ArrayMesh on the worker corrupts RIDs under the headless dummy renderer, so workers ship raw arrays and the main thread creates the mesh in `apply()` — sub-millisecond for a 65×65 chunk. Measured over a 2 km run: worst install cost well under the 4 ms gate, chunk count plateaus, memory flat.

**2026-08-13 — Deep sand costs speed and jump height, and the feet visibly settle. All three key off `get_sand_depth`.**
Joshua wanted depth to be felt, not just seen. The speed multiplier lives in `_target_speed()` — one hook covers walk, run and crouch, and because the animation blends off the speed actually reached, the stride slows to match for free. Effects scale linearly to full strength at `deep_sand_depth` 0.4 m: speed ×0.6, jump height ×0.75, and the mesh settles by 35% of local depth capped at 0.12 m (composed with the stair-smoothing offset; `_settle_step` is the single writer of the visual's height). With no terrain injected the depth reads 0 and every hook is exactly inert — the graybox plays identically, which `verify_player`'s full-speed measurements prove each run. Measured on the real desert by `verify_terrain --sand`: 1.08 m/s walking through 0.4 m+ sand vs 1.80 m/s on hard ground, sink settling at exactly the cap.

**2026-08-13 — verify_player moved to the graybox's west-edge lane (x = −40) for its flat-ground modes.**
Its gait and feel numbers assumed world.tscn's flat plane, which is now dunes. The graybox's west edge is 85 m of clear flat runway, and its terrain-less LevelRoot doubles as the proof that the sand hooks stay inert off-terrain. All modes re-passed with the Phase 3 numbers unchanged: walk launch 0.15 s, run wind-up 0.38 s, reversal 0.38 s, foot slip 14%, stairs rendering a 0.063 m worst one-tick move.

**2026-08-13 — Camera pitch lowered 45° → 19°. Joshua's pick from a seven-rung comparison; supersedes the 45° entry above and PLAN's fixed decision.**
Joshua asked for a more horizontal camera with dunes in the background and a sliver of horizon, on real terrain. A geometry finding shaped the choice: with the narrow 35° diorama lens, the top of frame at 32° pitch still points 14° below horizontal, so *no* background exists above ~26° — "a little flatter" in feel means 22–16°, not 38°. Screenshots at 45/38/32/26/22/19/16° from the same dune hollow (tools/shoot_pitch.gd) went to Joshua; he picked 19°: rolling dune backs across the top of frame, the haze band above them, and the player still almost never blocked — at 16° a crest occasionally hides you in hollows, and flatter than ~15° it would happen constantly. Pitch stays one export var; 16° is a number change away if the extra drama ever wins.

**2026-08-13 — The flatter camera brings load radius 5 (11×11 chunks) and a warm distance haze. Supersedes the "no far-field" conclusion above.**
That entry's math was true at 45° and died with it: at 19° the top of frame reaches ground ~400 m out at max zoom. The loaded grid grows to 11×11 (121 chunks — measured, still trivial for streaming and framerate), and an exponential warm haze (`fog_density 0.004`, colour = the sky's horizon cream, `fog_sky_affect 0`) melts the far dunes into the horizon well before the loaded edge. No backdrop, no skirt — the horizon is real streamed terrain dissolving into atmosphere, which also reads as desert heat. Haze values live in desert_environment.tscn; Phase 7's lighting pass owns their final tuning.

**2026-08-13 — The camera keeps a minimum clearance above the sand: a vertical lift, not the rejected ducking.**
At 19° the camera rides only ~8 m above the player's ground, so a tall dune behind the player could swallow it — and terrain deliberately never fades. The rig samples the terrain's analytic height at the camera's own footprint each tick (microseconds, no raycast) and lifts smoothly by just enough to keep `terrain_clearance` (1.2 m) of air. This is distinct from the camera-ducking Phase 2 rejected: framing distance never changes, the camera only rises — the same thing a crane operator would do. Inert on levels without terrain; `_follow_position` is tracked separately from the lifted position so the lift can never feed back into the follow damping.

## Phase 5 — sand footprint physics

**2026-08-14 — `deep_sand_depth` raised 0.4 → 1.5 m; the wading speed itself (×0.6) is untouched.**
Joshua's opening playtest of the real desert: the slowdown is fine, but it applies almost everywhere. Measured against the actual depth distribution (250k samples over 2 km²): at the 0.4 m threshold, 56% of the desert sat at the *full* ×0.6 penalty and the median sand depth out there is 0.5 m — "deep sand" was the default terrain, not the exception. Raising the saturation depth to 1.5 m reserves full wading for genuine dune bodies (20% of area), leaves flats and skirts barely touched, and lifts the average speed multiplier from 0.73 to 0.83. Chosen over softening the ×0.6 itself because Joshua explicitly said the speed was fine if deep sand were rarer; jump softening and foot-sink scale off the same factor, so everything stays coherent. He played the new value and approved. Next notch if ever wanted: threshold 2.0 m, or ×0.7 at depth. (The 19° camera and its slope behaviour were approved in the same playtest.)

**2026-08-14 — Stamps come from the toe bones via a FootstepStamper child, wired player → level → sand as signals.**
The prototype's distance-clocked dots are replaced by watching the skeleton's `LeftToeBase`/`RightToeBase`: a swinging foot travels roughly twice body speed and a planted one nearly stops, so a foot that drops below half of body speed has planted — stamp it once, exactly where the bone is. The "sand stamper interface" the plan asked for is deliberately thin: stampers emit a `stamped(pos, radius, strength, angle, stretch)` signal, the player re-raises its child's signal, and the level connects that to `SandDeformation.stamp()` — so the camel in Phase 6 just needs its own stamper child and one `connect`. Deep-sand wading adds a faint drag stamp between footfalls; landings splat off the existing `landed` signal. Crouch-sliding feet never "plant", so crouching marks nothing — consistent with the known crouch-walk gap.

**2026-08-14 — Deformation texture: 2048² over a 128 m region (16 texels/m), after 1024 proved to alias.**
At 8 texels/m a footprint (0.16 m across-radius) spans ~2.6 px, and whether its core survives rasterisation depends on sub-pixel phase — verify_prints showed strict alternation of readable/invisible prints as the stride walked in and out of alignment with the texel grid. 16 texels/m gives a print ~5 px and every stamp lands. Cost: two RGBA16F 2048² targets ≈ 64 MB VRAM, and the worst main-thread pass measured 0.8 ms (budget 1 ms) — the pass itself is one full-screen blit the M2 Pro doesn't notice.

**2026-08-14 — Prints darken multiplicatively; print depth caps at 0.3 m of sand, not the colour ramp's 4 m.**
Two prototype findings. Tinting *toward* `sand_shadow` vanished exactly where sand is thin, because the colour ramp already paints thin sand nearly that colour — prints now multiply albedo by a shadow/light ratio, visibly darker on every base tone. And vertex `COLOR.a` normalises depth against the ramp's 4 m, which made a print in 0.65 m of perfectly adequate sand render at 16% strength — the shader rescales the cap so prints saturate at `print_full_depth` 0.3 m. The deep → faint → gone gradient the gate asks for lives in the 0–0.3 m band, which is where the flats-to-dune transitions actually are.

**2026-08-14 — Wind leans on the prints as whole-texel drift, and the verifier had to learn prints move by design.**
`wind_drift_per_minute` (0.5 m default) migrates the whole accumulation downwind along the terrain's `wind_yaw_degrees`, accumulated sub-texel and applied in whole texels so every copy stays exact — old trails smear the way the dunes lie, and a future real wind system replaces one constant. Debugging lesson recorded for posterity: a long "texture randomly wipes" chase concluded that recentring, ping-pong and decay were all exact — the "lost" prints had simply drifted off the fixed world points the test probed. verify_prints now tests drift separately and disables it for the stay-put checks.

**2026-08-14 — The print system is biome-ready by contract; the deformation texture's G channel is reserved for future material classes.**
Joshua plans multiple biomes (sand, mud, snow) and asked whether prints will confine themselves to ground where they make sense. They already do, structurally: the deformation texture stores only a material-blind "how pressed" value; where prints may appear is gated per-vertex by COLOR.a = depth of depressible material (a mud biome writes mud depth there and rock refuses prints automatically); stampers emit position/pressure and never know the surface; and every look/feel knob (tint, depth, fade, wind drift) is a uniform, so per-biome materials are number changes. The one thing one shared texture cannot express is two decay rates in one region (a trail crossing sand into mud should heal at different speeds) — the fix is a material-class value in the texture's unused G channel, so that channel is now *reserved*: future sessions must not repurpose it. No biome code exists yet and none should until biomes are a real phase.

**2026-08-14 — Footprint look signed off: darkness 0.55, depth 0.12 m, fade 180 s. Phase 5 gate closed.**
Joshua picked from the Footprint Ladder (same trail re-shot at each candidate value — the flip-page pattern from earlier phases) and kept the working defaults; fade time approved from play. The circle-walk gate item was closed with scripted evidence: an octagon loop on deep sand already shows a fresh-to-old gradient at the moment of return, and 90 s later the loop is mostly refilled with its remnant drifted downwind. verify_player re-run after the stamper hook went into player.gd: foot slip still 13%, all animation states correct — the watcher is passive.

## Phase 6 — environment assets (Part 1: house & interior systems)

**2026-08-14 — Phase 6 splits into two parts: the house and the building systems first, the camel second.**
Joshua's call at the planning session. The camel needs a Meshy generation he hasn't made yet, and the house carries the systems everything later depends on (enterable interiors, prop reuse, POI ground). Splitting means Part 1 could be finished and reviewed rather than half-built while waiting on an asset. Both parts share `feature/phase-6-environment`; the phase gate closes after Part 2.

**2026-08-14 — The interior is enterable now, with a cutaway, instead of the "blocked door" PLAN originally scoped.**
Joshua chose it. The cost is one reusable node rather than a house feature, and the payoff is that every future building gets an interior for free — where a blocked door would have had to be reopened later anyway, after the walls were already built assuming nobody goes in.

**2026-08-14 — The house is generated by a committed script, not modelled by hand in the GUI.**
`tools/build_house.py` builds it from boxes. Same reasoning as `build_player.py`: the export stays reproducible, and the quality gate's "re-export reproduces the asset" claim stays true. It also suits the subject — a flat-shaded low-poly adobe building genuinely is a pile of right angles, so procedural geometry costs nothing in expressiveness, and every dimension becomes a named constant that the Godot side can be checked against. The wall tone is a single constant with a `--wall <palette>` override that renders a comparison ladder without touching the committed asset, for the one choice that is Joshua's rather than the code's.

**2026-08-14 — Collision comes from Godot's glTF `-col` name suffix, not hand-authored collision boxes.**
The importer generates a StaticBody3D with a trimesh shape from the very mesh being drawn, so collision cannot drift from the geometry, and nobody transcribes forty box transforms into a .tscn — where, per CLAUDE.md, a row-major `Transform3D` typo yields collision that is wrong in a way screenshots never show. Static architecture makes the trimesh free. One consequence had to be fixed: the importer hangs the StaticBody3D *under* the MeshInstance3D, and `occluder_fader.gd`'s `_visuals_of` walked the body, its children and its parent's children, but never the parent itself — so nothing imported this way could fade. Its comment claimed it handled "either way round"; now it does.

**2026-08-14 — The house is emitted as one mesh object per wall side per storey.**
`OccluderFader` fades a whole `GeometryInstance3D`. With the building as one mesh, standing behind it would ghost the entire house — far wall, floor and all — which inside a room reads as translucent mush. One object per side means only the wall actually between camera and player ghosts. It costs a dozen static bodies on a single building, which is nothing.

**2026-08-14 — `InteriorCutaway` cuts at a plane above the occupant's head, rather than hiding a configured list of "the storey above".**
The plane comes from the occupant's own Y, so one node handles a building of any height with nothing per-storey to configure: parts wholly above it disappear, parts it passes through — the walls of the storey you are standing in — ghost to a readable outline, parts below stay solid. Walk upstairs and the plane rises with you, so the roof goes and the floor you just left becomes solid again, automatically. The rejected alternative was exported per-storey lists of node paths, which GDScript cannot express as an array of arrays without a resource per storey, and which would have to be re-authored for every building.

**2026-08-14 — Exactly one system may write `transparency` on any given part, decided statically by collision layer.**
`OccluderFader` and `InteriorCutaway` both animate `GeometryInstance3D.transparency`; two writers on one property fight every frame and flicker. So the house's parts are partitioned: the cutaway's (upper slab, upper walls, roof, and every furnishing) sit on the world layer alone, invisible to the fader's layer-3 mask; the fader's (ground walls, stair, awning) sit on layer 5. `tools/verify_house.gd` asserts the partition, because it is the kind of rule a later prop would break silently.

**2026-08-14 — Every interior item is an independent asset with its own .blend and .glb.**
Joshua's requirement, and the right one. A bed baked into `house.glb` could only ever be in that house, could never become interactable on its own, and would turn "move the table" into a re-export. As ten separate assets they drop into any building we make later, and furnishing a room is a scene edit. `tools/build_furnishings.py` builds all ten; `tools/lowpoly.py` holds the box/drum/profile builder the house and the furnishings now share — the second real user that CLAUDE.md asks for before extracting.

**2026-08-14 — Hand-made assets get their palette colours by mapping the .glb's materials to the palette .tres files at import, not by exporting colours from Blender.**
ART_DIRECTION says hand-modelled assets use `resources/palette/`; the surest way to honour that is to let the palette files *be* the materials. Godot's scene importer supports it per material, so a glb whose materials are named `clay`, `wood` and so on is wired straight through by `tools/map_palette_materials.py`. Retuning a palette colour then reaches every building and prop at once with no re-export, and an asset can never drift a few percent off-palette through a colour-space mistake in the export path — the Blender-side colours only ever drive Blender's viewport. (The player is deliberately skipped: it keeps its baked texture under the Phase 3 exception.)

**2026-08-14 — The homestead stands on a pad levelled into the analytic terrain, at a site the seed chooses.**
`TerrainSettings.setup` scans a seeded annulus 45–110 m out, scoring each candidate by the largest height step its blend band would have to absorb, and levels the winner. Because the mesh, `HeightMapShape3D` collision, spawn seating, footprint depth capping and the camera's terrain lift all sample the same analytic functions, every one of them inherits the flattening for free — this is the "POI injection at generation time" hook ARCHITECTURE.md reserved. Choosing the flattest site rather than a fixed spot is what keeps the join walkable: the chosen site needs 11.5° against the 31° budget, where levelling a pad into a dune body would have walled it off. Sand on the pad is thinned to 0.25 m, which reads as packed courtyard earth and makes footprints fade out as you approach the door. `verify_terrain` gained a dense homestead audit, because the pad is a couple of hundred square metres inside a 16 km² sample area and is the only terrain the project *edits* rather than generates.

**2026-08-14 — Stair treads must exceed ~0.41 m, and this is a constraint on every staircase we ever build.**
The player's `_try_step_up` finds the tread by raising the capsule and pushing it forward by its own radius plus the probe margin (0.35 + 0.06 m). If the next riser falls inside that reach, the probe hits it and concludes the step is a wall — so the player stops dead at the bottom of the flight. The house's first stair used a 0.34 m tread, which looks entirely normal and was completely unclimbable; the upper floor was unreachable and nothing about the geometry, the collision or a screenshot showed it. Treads are now 0.48 m (a 30° flight). Found only by `tools/verify_house.gd` walking the stair with real input, which is the argument for these harnesses in one example.

**2026-08-14 — The player now spawns in the homestead courtyard rather than at the world origin.**
The game should open looking at the one built thing in the desert. The spawn is a `Marker3D` inside `homestead.tscn`, so the scene that knows the building's layout decides where you arrive; `LevelRoot` takes its XZ and drops the player onto the surface as before.

**2026-08-14 — Known, unresolved: a bounded ObjectDB leak warning at exit once the house is in the world.**
2–3 plain `RefCounted` instances are reported leaked when the engine shuts down, and only when the house is present *and* the player spawns near it. Bisected to rule out: `house.gd`, `InteriorCutaway`, `OccluderFader` (it reproduces with the house on layer 1, which the fader cannot see), `SandDeformation`, LOD/shadow-mesh generation, and the chunk manager's results queue (cleared in `_exit_tree` anyway, which was a correct fix regardless). The count does not grow with run length — 2 at 200 frames, 3 at 800 — so it is a shutdown-ordering artifact rather than a runtime leak, with no gameplay effect. Recorded rather than chased further; revisit if it ever grows.

## Camera orbit (raised mid-Phase 6)

**2026-08-14 — The camera orbits 360° around the player on a left-click drag, with the pitch fixed. Supersedes "rotatable in 45° steps later if wanted; never free-look."**
Joshua asked for it during his first playtest of the house, and asked for it now rather than later — correctly, because every later judgement about how a building or a prop reads depends on being able to look at it from any side. He specified left-click drag and a fixed angle.

Free **yaw**, fixed **pitch**, is the whole shape of the decision. The pitch is the art direction — 19° is a value he picked from a seven-rung ladder, and it is what puts dune backs and a band of haze across the top of frame. Letting a drag change it would mean every screenshot from then on was taken at an arbitrary angle, and it would also let the camera be driven into the sand, which the terrain-clearance lift exists to prevent. So the vertical component of the drag is simply ignored, and `verify_player --orbit` asserts that it is.

Mechanics worth keeping: the drag is banked in `_unhandled_input` and applied once in `_physics_process`, so the rig stays on the same 60 Hz tick as the player it follows — physics interpolation then carries the rotation to the render rate, and there is no relative jitter between camera and character. It is applied whole, with no damping: a drag that lags behind the hand feels rubbery rather than smooth, and damping belongs on the follow, not on direct manipulation. The mouse is *captured* for the duration of the drag rather than merely hidden, so a long spin never runs out of desk or slides off the window, and the cursor is warped back to where it was grabbed on release. Losing window focus mid-drag ends the drag, since the button-up would otherwise land in another application and leave the mouse captured with no way out.

Direction is a taste call with two defensible conventions (mouse-look versus grab-and-spin-the-world), so it is `invert_orbit`, an export, defaulting to mouse-look: drag right, view turns right.

The real risk was never the rotation but the handoff. Movement is camera-relative through `yaw_changed` → `player.set_view_yaw`, and a sign error there looks perfect standing still and sends you backwards the moment you turn around. `verify_player --orbit` walks the player at four bearings and asserts "forward" is within 0.001 of away-from-camera at each; it also asserts that a 720 px drag produces exactly the yaw the sensitivity implies, which is what catches the value being applied per motion event instead of per tick.

**2026-08-14 — The interior floor stands 0.12 m proud of the ground, and ripples are suppressed on the homestead pad.**
Joshua reported the ground floor "is sand", and it still was after its material had been changed to match the upper storey — because the material was never the problem. The slab sat flush with the terrain, and measuring it showed the desert standing *above* the floor at 193 of 195 points across the footprint, by up to 6.8 cm: the sand mesh was simply drawn over the floor. Levelling the pad had flattened the base height and the sand depth but left the ripple field running through it, which is nothing in open desert and everything under a flat-bottomed slab. So ripples now fade out with the same homestead weight (pad relief 0.084 m → 0.000 m), and the floor is lifted 0.12 m regardless, since a floor exactly level with the ground z-fights with it and a building wants a plinth anyway.

The knock-on caught the same session, and it is the more interesting half: **lifting the floor shut the front door.** Crossing a threshold makes the controller raise the capsule by `max_step_height` and push it forward to feel for the tread, so a doorway needs to clear the player's 1.75 m *plus* that 0.35 m lift above the threshold. At 2.10 m the door had 1.94 m of clear height under its lintel — fine over a flush floor, and impassable the moment there was a step. `DOOR_H` is now 2.40 m. Same shape of failure as the 0.34 m stair treads: a dimension that looks entirely reasonable, is silently coupled to the character controller, and produces geometry you cannot enter. Both rules are now in ART_DIRECTION, and `verify_house` asserts the floor clears the sand so this one cannot come back quietly.

**2026-08-14 — The step-up probe runs against a body 7 cm slimmer than the one that collides. This is the "hung up on ledges" fix.**
Joshua reported getting caught half way up the staircase and on the raised threshold, and offered three theories: a building-placement system, levelling the sand under buildings, or resizing ledges for the character. The third was right, and there was a fourth underneath it.

Sweeping the approach across lanes rather than walking it once down the middle is what found it. A doorway with 0.20 m of geometric slack had **0.12 m you could actually walk through**; the stair's usable lane was 0.50 m of a 1.30 m flight. The missing width was contact the body was merely *sliding along*: `_try_step_up` answers "is there a step ahead of me?" by sweeping the capsule forward, and at full width anything brushing the body's side answers that question too. Graze a door jamb, or the house wall the outside stair runs against, and the step reads as a wall — the player stops dead for no reason they can see. It is worst exactly where Joshua hit it, because a doorway and a stair are the two places you are near a wall *and* stepping up at the same time.

The probe now runs with the capsule radius temporarily reduced by `step_probe_slim` (0.07 m) and its reach widened by the same amount, so it meets a real obstacle at exactly the distance it always did while ignoring contact it is only sliding along. Measured after: every doorway lane passes, and the usable band went 0.12 m → 0.50 m. All of Phase 3's movement numbers are unchanged — foot slip 15%/13%, the graybox staircase still renders a 0.063 m worst one-tick move with zero fall-animation ticks, and every graybox fixture behaves as designed.

The geometry was widened too, since it was genuinely tight for the character: `DOOR_W` 1.10 → 1.30 m and `STAIR_W` 1.30 → 1.50 m. Both fixes matter — the controller one because it would otherwise recur on every building we ever make, the geometric one because a cozy game should not ask for precision walking.

`verify_house` now sweeps four approach lanes at both the door and the stair and fails if any lane cannot get through, so "it works if you aim perfectly" can never again pass as working. Joshua's other two theories were already covered: the homestead pad is levelled and, since the floor fix, perfectly flat — neither was the cause.

## Doors & interaction (Phase 6 continuation)

**2026-08-14 — Interaction is a first-class two-part system: `Interactable` on the prop, `Interactor` on the player. Joshua's requirement: a built-in mechanic, not door code.**
The prop side is an `Area3D` component (`scenes/props/interactable.gd`) carrying a prompt string and an `interacted(actor)` signal — the same one-signal-one-connect shape as Phase 5's stamper interface. The player side (`scenes/player/interactor.gd`) is a self-contained child of the player scene that overlaps those volumes, floats a "E — Open" `Label3D` over the nearest one, and calls `interact()` on it when E is pressed. `player.gd` was not touched, no autoload was added, and neither half knows the other's internals — a chest, a lamp or a camel saddle becomes usable by adding one child and connecting one signal. Interaction volumes live alone on **physics layer 4**, so they can never block movement, never fade, and the overlap query can never see a wall; `verify_house` asserts the layer.

**2026-08-14 — The door is one reusable prop (`scenes/props/door/`), not house geometry.**
`tools/build_door.py` builds the leaf with its origin on the hinge line, sized to the standard doorway from `build_house.py`, so the scene swings the whole model by rotating one node. Collision is a hand-authored `BoxShape3D` on an `AnimatableBody3D` — the one place the `-col` trimesh convention is deliberately not used, because a door moves and a box is the honest shape of a leaf. The door swings **away from whoever opens it** (the sign of the actor's hinge-local Z picks the direction), so opening can never shove the player; E toggles it and nothing auto-closes. Both house doors are instances of the same scene; every future building gets its doors the same way.

**2026-08-14 — Doors adopt the occlusion management of the wall they are mounted in.**
The single-writer rule partitions the house between `OccluderFader` and `InteriorCutaway` by layer, and a door sits *in* a wall owned by one of them. So the front door keeps the fader's layer 5 like the ground walls around it, while the upper door's instance sets `fadeable` off and `house.gd` hands its leaf to the cutaway — otherwise the upper storey would fade away and leave a door floating in the air. `verify_house`'s layer audit covers both doors.

**2026-08-14 — Engine trap, recorded loudly: `AnimatableBody3D` defaults to `sync_to_physics = true`, which only tracks *local* transform changes.**
With the default on, the door collided perfectly in a standalone scene and was intangible in the game: `LevelRoot` moves the whole homestead onto the terrain pad after instancing, an ancestor move the sync mode never propagates, so the leaf's physics body stayed ninety metres from its mesh. Raycasts through the doorway (debug probes) hit it at its stale location; the player walked through the visible door. `door.tscn` sets `sync_to_physics = false`, which makes the body follow ancestor moves exactly like the walls' imported `StaticBody3D`s do, and a swinging leaf still pushes the player by ordinary depenetration. Any future moving collider under anything the level relocates needs the same flag.

**2026-08-14 — No on-screen interact prompt. Joshua's call: players know how to interact with things.**
The floating "E — Open" label shipped with the first door and came straight back out: it read as UI clutter in a game whose whole screen is meant to be desert. The `Interactable.prompt` string ("Open"/"Close") stays maintained even though nothing renders it — the verify harness asserts against it, and it is the ready-made hook if an accessibility or first-time-hints toggle ever wants to draw prompts again. The affordance for "this door works" is the door itself: leaf, pull, and the E convention.

**2026-08-14 — The step probe measures in up to three directions, and a low-momentum step also advances the body. This is the second "hung up on ledges and stairs" fix, and again the measurement pointed somewhere unexpected.**
A twelve-scenario sweep around the homestead found the doorway clean at every angle but the stair broken for anything except a dead-straight lane: an approach 15–20° off either deflected clean off the flight (rise 0.00 over six seconds of held input) or jammed forever at the stair's foot, wedged in the corner against the house wall or the parapet.

Two mechanisms, both now fixed in `player.gd`:

*Direction.* The probe asked "is there a step in front of me?" only along the **velocity** — and the moment `move_and_slide` starts sliding you along a riser, velocity *is* the riser's direction, so the question was never asked about the thing actually in the way. The probe now asks along the input direction first (what the player wants), velocity second, and then — when both read a wall — **square against each steep face the sweeps met**. Perpendicular to the face is the one direction the reach geometry is honest in: a diagonal approach sweeps a longer path over the tread and clips the next riser or a flanking wall, which is how a climbable flight was being reported as a wall. Collecting *all* contact faces (not just the first) matters in corners, where the sweep meets the unclimbable parapet *and* the climbable riser; and the face fallback is gated on the input pushing into the face (`dot > 0.4`), so walking past a curb while brushing it never climbs it — asserted by the graze scenario.

*Momentum.* With those fixes the jammed player measured the step correctly, stepped up — and slid straight back down, nineteen times at the same coordinates: raising the body leaves it perched on the tread's lip over its old footing, and pressed into a corner there is no momentum to carry it onto the tread before it slips off. So a step taken below 1.0 m/s of planar speed also **advances the body one capsule-radius along the measured direction** — precisely the spot the measurement's own drop-test proved has tread under it and clear space above. The visual absorbs the shift through the same mechanism that absorbs the vertical pop (now a world-space `_step_shift`), and walking-pace climbs never trigger it, so every Phase 3 number is unchanged: reversal 0.38 s, rendered worst one-tick move 0.063 m, zero fall-animation ticks on the graybox stair.

`verify_house` gained `_stair_drift_climbs`: six angled and wall-pressed climbs (±15° and ±20° entries, ±10° bias against wall and parapet) that must all reach the upper floor. All six pass; before the fixes, four of the six failed.

**2026-08-14 — The stair parapet's top has a knee at the stair head. The last two drift failures were not the controller at all.**
The +15°/+20° drift climbs kept ending at rise 0.00 even after the probe fixes — and the trace showed why: the player was *summiting the flight and then walking over the parapet and falling off its outside*. The parapet top was drawn as one straight line from bottom-rail to the far end of the landing, but the stair finishes rising earlier than that, so near the head the "rail" sagged to ~0.15 m above the treads — which to the step-up probe is simply a step, and it is right. The profile now runs at tread + 0.85 m up the flight and level along the landing. The general rule — a rail must hold more than `max_step_height` over every surface it actually runs beside, measured against treads rather than a slope line — is recorded in ART_DIRECTION's modelling rules, because it is the third time a plausible-looking dimension silently coupled to the character controller (0.34 m treads, the 2.10 m lintel over a raised threshold, now this).

## Desert restyle — Shaybah look (Phase 6 interlude)

**2026-08-14 — The sand palette is now Shaybah orange: `sand_light #EFA254`, `sand_mid #D97E2E`, `sand_shadow #A85419`. Joshua's pick from a four-rung in-game ladder against his Shaybah reference photo (`docs/references/shaybah_dunes.jpg`).**
The trios were sampled from the photo and lifted in steps back toward the old cream — raw photo pixels (~#B14201 on sunlit faces) are exposure-dark and would muddy under our sun and haze. "Shaybah" beat a warmer half-step ("amber") and a photo-deep rung ("ember"). Everything downstream re-derives at runtime: the vertex-color ramp, the footprint compaction tint (shadow/light ratio) and the courtyard all follow the three .tres files. Props and buildings keep their own baked materials — the homestead deserves one cohesion look at the Phase 6 gate, since awning and cushions were chosen against cream sand. Side benefit, long promised: the pale traveler finally reads against the ground at gameplay distance.

**2026-08-14 — Wind ripples are shading, not geometry: an analytic asymmetric wave in the wind frame perturbs the facet normal per-pixel and darkens trough lines, in `sand_terrain.gdshader`. Joshua picked the middle "crisp" rung (strength 0.08 m, wavelength 2.4 m, trough tint 0.12).**
The 1 m terrain mesh can never hold a 2.4 m wave as geometry without aliasing, and displacing vertices would desynchronise collision from visuals — the Phase 4 gate's hardest-won property. Shading sidesteps both: each ridge catches the real sun on its windward slope and shades its lee, so ripples respond to the camera orbit like everything else, stay crisp at any wavelength, and cost a few ALU ops. They lie across the same `wind_yaw_degrees` that stretches the dunes (ChunkManager pushes the direction uniform), fade out where sand is thin (vertex `COLOR.a`), inside footprint depressions (`1 - pressed`), and past ~55 m of view distance where they would only shimmer. `ripple_strength = 0` disables the entire path.

**2026-08-14 — Windowed verify runs are untrustworthy while a second Godot instance is on screen: the focus dance can promote the wrong window.**
